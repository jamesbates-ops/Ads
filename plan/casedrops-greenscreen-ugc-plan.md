# Casedrops green screen UGC: Raptor & Crimson and Dolum Estates

Format: single presenter talking straight to camera against a chroma-key green screen, vertical 9:16, 720p (Kling 3.0, standard mode, native audio). Locked-off camera, no cuts inside a clip. B-roll, product shots and text overlays are added by the editors over the keyed background.

Presenter: the supplied reference image (grey swept-back hair, round tortoiseshell glasses, light-blue linen shirt). Every clip uses that image as its start frame, so each one opens on the same pose and the cuts between clips read as standard UGC jump cuts.

Voice: Higgsfield preset voice **Grady** on every clip. Kling's native voice drifted in accent from clip to clip, so each Kling clip was run through Higgsfield voice change to Grady. Voice change keeps Kling's timing, lip sync and the words exactly as Kling spoke them, so a word Kling mispronounces stays mispronounced and needs a new Kling take.

Audio: diegetic only. Close mic in a small treated studio: clean voice, a faint hint of room, very low room tone, light clothing rustle on gestures. No music or SFX.

## The two scripts

The brief mixed both ads together using `//` as a separator. Split out, each ad is one of the three hooks followed by its own body.

- H2: "This is why you should always do your research."

### Changes from the brief

The opening lines (RC1, D1) are word for word from the brief, as requested. Flagged for the claims check: 6 × $80 = $480, which isn't "over $500", and "the same exact bottle for around 100 bucks" sits oddly next to "$17 a bottle". The only other changes:

| Ad | Brief said | Generated as | Why |

## QC (transcribed with Whisper, frames checked)

- **Picture:** same presenter, framing and green screen in every clip. Hands come into frame for measured, natural gestures and the expression settles from the reference smile into a composed delivery. No on-screen text. The green screen stays clean and even.
- **Audio:** no clipped first words (clip heads sit at -44 to -60 dB). Tails carry low room tone (about -55 to -60 dB) rather than digital silence. No music.
- **H2:** about 1.3s pause in the middle of the sentence ("…should always | do your research"). Close it with a cut.
- **D1:** two transcription passes hear "I just saw *the hundred* selling…" where the script says "they're selling" (around 10.2–10.6s). If it sounds wrong, patch steps 2–3 with R1's take of the identical words: D1 0.00 → 8.70, then R1 8.10 → 11.95 ("and I just saw they're selling the same exact bundle for"), then D1 12.45 → 15.04 ("around a hundred bucks").
- **R2:** "cassis" transcribes as "Cassius", so check it by ear. D2 has an alternate take of the whole tasting-notes sentence from 6.60s on, but its "vanilla" sounds like "manila".
- **Caymus:** check the pronunciation (KAY-mus) in R2 and D2. Whisper can't confirm it either way.

## Notes for the edit

- **Disclosure:** the presenter is AI-generated and gives a first-person purchase story. Add an "AI-generated presenter / dramatisation" label on screen so the ad isn't read as a real customer testimonial (FTC rules on consumer reviews and testimonials; CAP code in the UK).
- **Claims to confirm before launch:** "95 points", "top 4% of all wines worldwide", the tasting notes and the 5-year ageing line are shared by both ads. "Rated higher than Caymus / Opus One / Ridge" are comparative claims about named competitors and need evidence on file. The Ridge producer trades as Ridge Vineyards; the script line was kept as written ("Ridge Estates").
- **Voice continuity:** every clip gets its own native Kling voice. The voice is prompted the same way each time, but timbre can drift a little from clip to clip, so check the voice matches across each assembled cut. If one clip is off, regenerate just that clip; don't redo the set.
- **Generation settings:** `kling3_0`, mode `std` (720p), sound on, 9:16, the reference image as `start_image`. Each prompt repeats the same staging, voice and studio-audio description and adds one gesture cue for that line. Cost was about 1.75 credits per second, roughly 245 credits for all 12 clips.
